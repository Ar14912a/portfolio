      What each slider does

      Move a slider and its line lights up. These lines follow the results of my research.

      Ion temperature gradient (ηi) up. This is the free energy that drives the mode. The paper reports that below ηi = 2/3 the mode is stable. Above it the peak growth rate rises strongly, the unstable range widens and the peak moves slightly to larger k. Solitons get a smaller amplitude and their width barely changes (Fig. 7). In a machine this means stronger turbulence.

      Temperature ratio (τ = Te/Ti) up. The paper finds that the growth rate falls and the unstable range narrows, so hotter electrons calm the mode. The soliton gets taller (0.009 to 0.072 in its Fig. 8) and narrower.

      Magnetic field (B0) up. The paper reports that the growth rate falls, the unstable range narrows and the peak moves slightly to smaller k, so a stronger field stabilises the mode. Solitons get narrower and more localised (Fig. 6).

      Spectral index (κ) down. More fast, suprathermal electrons are present. In the paper’s soliton figure (Fig. 5) the amplitude falls from 0.221 at κ = 100 to 0.045 at κ = 2. For large κ the Maxwellian result returns.

      Why it matters. Turbulence moves heat out of the plasma and weakens confinement. Choosing the right ηi, τ and B0 can reduce the ITG turbulence and improve confinement.
